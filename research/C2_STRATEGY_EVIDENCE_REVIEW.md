# C.2 Strategy and Mandate Evidence Review

**Review date:** 2026-11-23  
**Holdings transition under review:** 2025-09-30 to 2025-12-31  
**Cohort:** Six predeclared active-management funds  
**Evidence standard:** SEC prospectuses, prospectus supplements, shareholder reports, and contemporaneous fund disclosures. This is a documentary classification, not an independent audit of actual portfolio-manager behavior.

## Executive findings

The six funds are all actively managed, but the original growth/value/blend buckets are too coarse. The contemporaneous documents support two explicit growth mandates, two explicit value mandates, and two flexible growth/value mandates. Fidelity Magellan should not be described as a conventional blend fund: its prospectus says the adviser is not constrained by a particular investment style. Fidelity Contrafund also has explicit flexibility to invest in growth, value, or both.

Two important structural events need to accompany any interpretation of the 2025 transition:

1. **JPMorgan Large Cap Growth and JPMorgan Large Cap Value became non-diversified funds effective March 5, 2025.** This preceded the September–December observation window, so both endpoints in the C.2 transition occur after the change. It is not a within-window break, but it changes the legal concentration framework relative to earlier historical periods.
2. **Optimum Large Cap Growth experienced an ownership/control and advisory-agreement transition effective December 1, 2025, not a change in the named investment manager.** The SEC information statement identifies Delaware Management Company (DMC) as the investment manager; DMC became a series of Nomura Investment Management Business Trust following Nomura's acquisition of the relevant Macquarie asset-management business. The prior advisory agreement automatically terminated and a new DMC agreement became effective December 1. American Century and Los Angeles Capital continued as sub-advisers under new agreements. Official monthly disclosures show a stable aggregate allocation split, but do not establish unchanged security-level sleeve holdings or day-to-day decisions.

These findings do not invalidate the mechanical calculation. They do limit claims about stable management arrangements, strategy continuity, and attribution of the portfolio-change residual.

## Classification recommendation

Use the following descriptive groups in the cohort documentation:

- **Growth:** Optimum Large Cap Growth; JPMorgan Large Cap Growth.
- **Value:** JPMorgan Large Cap Value; Oakmark.
- **Flexible growth/value:** Fidelity Contrafund; Fidelity Magellan.

These are documentary strategy groupings, not Morningstar style-box classifications, and should not be used as a claim that each fund's realized portfolio exposures stayed inside a fixed style box during the quarter.

## Implications for interpreting the C.2 results

- Keep all six funds in the predeclared feasibility cohort. Do not silently reselect or remove a fund after seeing the results.
- Do not mark Optimum as a change in the named investment manager: DMC remained the manager. Record the December 1, 2025 advisory-agreement replacement and change in ultimate ownership/control separately. American Century and Los Angeles Capital continued, and published aggregate allocation weights were stable; this does not establish unchanged day-to-day portfolio decisions.
- Mark both JPMorgan funds as non-diversified from March 5, 2025. This is a pre-window structural change, not a September–December change.
- Do not call Contrafund or Magellan “blend” without qualification. “Flexible growth/value” is more faithful to their prospectuses.
- Keep the portfolio-change residual descriptive. It is not a causal estimate of manager trading and may reflect flows, corporate actions, manager/adviser changes, derivatives/cash, and reporting differences.
- The review does not satisfy a broader historical strategy-continuity audit. Before making longer-horizon comparisons, obtain earlier prospectuses and dated supplements for each fund and record manager/sub-adviser allocation changes.

## Dated portfolio-manager roster and responsibility comparison

The March 31, 2025 prospectus/SAI disclosure (pre-transition) and the March 31, 2026 prospectus/SAI disclosure (post-transition) list the same six portfolio managers for the two Optimum Large Cap Growth Fund sub-adviser sleeves. All six retain the same stated fund start dates. The filings continue to assign day-to-day management of each sleeve to the respective sub-adviser teams.

| Sub-adviser | Portfolio manager | 2025 title | 2026 title | Fund start | Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| American Century | Keith Lee, CFA | SVP; Senior PM; Co-CIO, Global Growth Equity | Same | July 2022 | Unchanged |
| American Century | Jeff Bourke, CFA | VP; Portfolio Manager | VP; Senior Portfolio Manager | July 2022 | Title updated; same manager |
| American Century | Tong Li | Portfolio Manager; Senior Quantitative Analyst | Same | July 2024 | Unchanged |
| Los Angeles Capital | Hal W. Reynolds, CFA | Vice Chair; Senior Portfolio Manager | Same | January 2023 | Unchanged |
| Los Angeles Capital | Daniel Arche, CFA | Director, Portfolio Strategy; Senior Portfolio Manager | Same | January 2023 | Unchanged |
| Los Angeles Capital | Daniel E. Allen, CFA | CEO; Senior Portfolio Manager | Same | January 2023 | Unchanged |

The responsibility descriptions also remain materially consistent. American Century's three named managers are described as primarily responsible for day-to-day management of its portion. Los Angeles Capital's three named managers are described as primarily responsible for its portion; the filing continues to describe Reynolds as overseeing the investment process, Arche as managing accounts under the firm's process and risk parameters, and Allen as a senior Portfolio Management team member and Investment Committee member.

The manager-level governance description remains consistent in substance: DMC has overall responsibility for general management, including selecting sub-advisers and monitoring their investment activity. The legal entity's trust affiliation changes from Macquarie Investment Management Business Trust to Nomura Investment Management Business Trust, but DMC remains the named investment manager.

**Interpretation:** this is positive documentary evidence of continuity in the disclosed sub-adviser portfolio-manager roster and stated role allocation across the transition. It is not proof that there were no temporary or unreported personnel changes, that actual responsibilities were performed identically, or that security-level decisions and trading processes were unchanged. The SAI's other-accounts tables are firm/manager-level snapshots with changed account counts and assets; they do not identify Optimum-specific decisions and should not be treated as direct evidence of continuity or discontinuity.

The formal flag therefore remains **day-to-day portfolio management change: not established**. A roster match is evidence about named personnel and disclosed responsibilities, not a substitute for trade-level or internal process records.
